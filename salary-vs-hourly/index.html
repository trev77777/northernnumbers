Salary vs Hourly in Canada: What Every Worker Should Know

🔄 Converting Salary to Hourly and Back

The simplest conversion is to divide annual salary by the number of paid hours in the year. In Canada, that is usually 2,080 hours for a 40-hour work week. But real life is rarely that simple. Vacation, statutory holidays, overtime rules, and employer-paid benefits all affect your true hourly value.

This calculator helps you see both sides: what hourly wage corresponds to a given salary, and what salary corresponds to a given hourly rate. It also shows how weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, and monthly pay compare so you can understand your actual take-home rhythm and budget accurately.

📅 Why Pay Periods Matter in Canada

In Canada, different employers use different pay periods: weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly. Your gross annual pay may be the same, but the number of pay periods changes your paycheck amount and the timing of bills. For example, a $100,000 salary is $3,846 per biweekly paycheque, but only $4,166 per semi-monthly paycheque.

Most people underestimate the difference between biweekly and semi-monthly pay. Biweekly pay gives two extra paycheques in the year, which can be helpful for managing holidays or savings. Semi-monthly pay is easier for monthly budgeting but can feel smaller on each paycheque. This calculator shows the exact breakdown so you can plan your cash flow with confidence.

🧳 Vacation and Stat Holiday Adjustments

The amount of paid vacation and stat holidays matters when comparing salary to hourly work. In Canada, most employees start with at least two weeks of paid vacation and 9–10 statutory holidays, depending on the province. If you are self-employed or a contractor, you need to earn additional hourly pay to cover that time off.

This page lets you select your paid vacation and statutory holidays so the result reflects your actual work year. For contractors, it also shows how a higher hourly rate may be necessary to compensate for unpaid time off and employer-provided benefits.

💼 Salary, Benefits, and Total Compensation

A salary offer is not just a number — it usually includes paid vacation, employer CPP/EI contributions, benefits, and sometimes bonuses or stock options. When you compare a salaried position with an hourly contract, remember to account for the value of employer-paid premiums and paid time off.

If you are evaluating a job offer or thinking about contracting, use this calculator along with a list of benefits. A $90,000 salary with health and dental, 3 weeks vacation, and employer pension contributions may be worth more than a $55/hr contract with no paid leave. The calculator gives you the base numbers; you should then add the value of benefits to the total compensation picture.

📈 Contractor Pricing and Self-Employment Realities

Contractors and self-employed Canadians need to set a higher hourly rate than a straight salary conversion suggests. This is because you are responsible for employer CPP, EI (if opted in), vacation time, sick days, equipment, liability insurance, and business expenses. A common guideline is to add 25–35% to the equivalent salaried hourly rate, but the actual premium depends on your industry and risk tolerance.

The calculator supports this by showing both standard and effective hourly values. Use it to compare a salaried job against contract work and make sure the hourly rate you quote covers your true cost of working and your desired lifestyle.

❓ Common Questions About Pay Conversions

How do I compare a bonus to my hourly rate?

To compare a bonus, convert the bonus into an equivalent hourly amount using the same number of paid hours in your year. For example, a $5,000 annual bonus on a 2,000-hour work year is worth $2.50 per hour. Use the calculator to see your base hourly value, then add the bonus value to compare total compensation accurately.

Should I include unpaid overtime?

No — this calculator assumes the hours entered are the hours you are paid for. If you regularly work unpaid overtime, your effective hourly wage is lower than the calculator's result. In that case, estimate your true annual paid hours and use that number instead of the standard 40 hours/week.

What if I’m paid salary but work part-time?

If you are salaried and work part-time hours, enter your actual annual salary and the hours you work each week. The result will show the equivalent hourly rate for your actual workload, which can help you compare part-time roles or decide whether a salaried position is worth the hours.
