🇨🇦 Key TFSA Rules Every Canadian Should Know

TFSA room accumulates every January 1 for every Canadian who is 18 or older and a resident of Canada — regardless of whether you actually have a TFSA account. You do not need to earn income to accumulate TFSA room (unlike RRSP). Room carries forward indefinitely — if you have never opened a TFSA and were 18 before 2009, you could have up to $109,000 in room available right now. Withdrawals restore to your room on January 1 of the following year, not immediately. This means if you withdraw $10,000 in October 2026, you cannot re-contribute it until January 1, 2027. Re-contributing the same year is one of the most common — and costly — TFSA mistakes.

⚠️ TFSA Over-Contribution Penalty

If you contribute more than your available TFSA room, the CRA charges a 1% per month penalty tax on the excess amount — every month the excess stays in the account. On a $10,000 over-contribution, that is $100 per month or $1,200 per year. The CRA sends a notice of assessment requiring you to withdraw the excess and pay the accumulated penalty. Always confirm your room in CRA My Account before making a large contribution. The most common cause of over-contributions is withdrawing and re-contributing in the same calendar year.

🌍 TFSA for Newcomers to Canada

If you immigrated to Canada after 2009, your TFSA room begins accumulating in the year you became a Canadian resident — not from 2009. For example, if you arrived in 2018 and are 18 or older, your room started in 2018. As of 2026, your maximum room would be $57,500 (sum of limits 2018–2026). You do not receive room for years you were not a Canadian resident. Non-residents can still hold a TFSA and make withdrawals, but contributions while non-resident are subject to a 1% monthly penalty tax regardless of available room.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find my exact TFSA room?

Log in to CRA My Account at canada.ca/my-cra-account. Under the TFSA section, you will see your current contribution room as of January 1 of the current year. Note that CRA's figure is based on information reported by financial institutions as of the prior year — very recent contributions or withdrawals may not yet be reflected. This calculator gives you an estimate — always confirm with CRA before making a large contribution.

Does TFSA room accumulate if I never opened a TFSA?

Yes. TFSA room accumulates automatically every January 1 for every eligible Canadian (18+, resident) regardless of whether you have a TFSA account. If you have never opened a TFSA and have been eligible since 2009, you have up to $109,000 in available room — you could contribute all of it immediately upon opening an account.

Can I contribute to my spouse's TFSA?

Yes. Unlike RRSPs, there is no spousal attribution rule for TFSAs. You can give money to your spouse to contribute to their own TFSA without any tax consequences, as long as they have the available room. The contribution uses their room, not yours. This is a common income-splitting strategy in retirement.

What is the TFSA limit for 2027?

The CRA has not yet announced the 2027 TFSA limit. The annual limit is indexed to inflation in $500 increments. Based on current inflation trends, the 2027 limit is expected to remain at $7,000, though it could increase to $7,500 if inflation warrants an adjustment. The CRA typically announces the following year's limit in the fall.
