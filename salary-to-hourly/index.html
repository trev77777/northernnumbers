⏰ Overtime Rules in Canada

Overtime rules in Canada are set provincially. Most provinces require overtime pay at 1.5× your regular rate after a weekly threshold. Ontario requires overtime after 44 hours per week (not 40 like the US). Federally regulated employees are subject to the Canada Labour Code, which requires overtime after 8 hours per day or 40 hours per week. Some provinces like Manitoba also have daily overtime thresholds. Overtime pay must be paid unless you have a written averaging agreement or modified work arrangement with your employer.

Salaried employees are also entitled to overtime in Canada — being paid a salary does not automatically exempt you. Managers and supervisors may be exempt if they have significant authority over other employees. If you are unsure whether you qualify for overtime, contact your provincial employment standards office.

💡 Salary vs Hourly: Which Is Better?

Whether salary or hourly pay is better depends on your situation. Salaried positions typically offer greater income predictability, benefits more often, and are more common in professional and managerial roles. Hourly positions offer more direct compensation for extra hours worked, can provide flexibility in scheduling, and are protected by provincial employment standards for overtime, public holidays, and minimum wage.

One important consideration: a $60,000 salary is not equivalent to a $60,000 annual income from an hourly role unless both involve the same number of hours. A salaried employee working 50 hours/week earns an effective hourly rate of $23.08 — far less than a $60,000-equivalent at 40 hours ($28.85/hr). Use this calculator to compare job offers by converting both to the same basis before deciding.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert a salary to an hourly rate in Canada?

Divide your annual salary by the number of hours you work per year. For a standard 40-hour work week over 52 weeks (2,080 hours), divide your salary by 2,080. A $70,000 salary works out to $33.65/hr. If you work fewer weeks due to unpaid leave, divide by the actual hours worked — a $70,000 salary over 50 weeks at 40 hours is $35.00/hr.

What is the difference between biweekly and semi-monthly pay?

Biweekly pay means you receive a paycheck every two weeks — 26 paychecks per year. Semi-monthly pay means twice a month — 24 paychecks per year. On a $60,000 salary, biweekly pay is $2,307.69 per check and semi-monthly is $2,500. Two months per year you receive three biweekly checks instead of two. Most Canadian employers use biweekly or semi-monthly payroll.

Does a salary include vacation pay in Canada?

For most salaried employees in Canada, vacation pay is included in your annual salary — you receive the same pay whether you are working or on vacation. For hourly employees, vacation pay is typically 4% of gross earnings (minimum in most provinces) paid out as a percentage of wages earned, either accrued with each paycheck or paid out when vacation is taken.
